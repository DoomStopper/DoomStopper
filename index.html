The Science

Dopamine is the chemical in your brain responsible for reward, essentially the chemical that tells your brain "do that again. Each time you scroll, your brain releases a spike of dopamine.

It works exactly like a slot machine: your brain is constantly anticipating a "win," so you keep pulling the lever.

How shorts hijack your brain

The Problem

Overwhelmed by this, the decision-making part of your brain goes quiet, leaving you to scroll mindlessly for hours without even realizing you're doing it.

Worse, your brain, overwhelmed by the amount of dopamine, kills off its own receptors, making normal everyday things feel much less interesting.

The Solution

DoomStopper works, not by simply blocking the app, (which would just encourage you to turn it off), but by introducing a gradual delay between shorts to break the dopamine loop, and snap you out of the scrolling trance.

@IFixIt
How to stop scrolling

How It Works

  • Starts Invisible: The first scroll delay is 0 seconds. You won't even notice it.
  • Gradual Friction: Every new Short adds a tiny 50-millisecond gray screen.
  • Unbothersome: It's slow enough that you won't notice it increasing video by video.

After about 20 videos you will begin to see the delay screen with stats about how much time the app has saved you.

What It Does

That gray screen acts as a pattern interrupt, it creates just enough empty space for your logical brain to wake up and ask, "Wait, am I actually enjoying this, or am I just scrolling?".

Additionally, a gray screen with numbers on it releases zero dopamine. It reduces the excitement of the scroll because your brain learns that swiping no longer equals an instant reward; instead, each scroll equals a boring period of waiting.

The Long Term

By increasing the wait time very slowly, each scroll releases a tiny bit less dopamine. Not enough that it feels overly frustrating, but just enough that your brain starts reviving those dead dopamine receptors, making normal, everyday hobbies feel fun again.
